# Competition rules
Effective 1 January 2027

These rules cover WACS leagues, cups, LANs and draft events. They add to the Code of conduct.

# 1. About competitions

1.1 Each event's page sets its format, dates, map pool, fee and match settings. Where the event page and this book differ, the event page wins.
1.2 Section 13 covers how LAN events differ from online play. The rest of this book applies at a LAN too.
1.3 A team's captain acts for the team. They sign it up, change its roster, reschedule its matches and run its veto. The coach can also reschedule matches, and run the veto in a league. What either agrees binds the team.
1.4 Every player and coach on a roster accepts these rules and the Code of conduct when the team signs up.
1.5 Captains must be in the WACS Discord, and answer staff during the event.
1.6 Staff announce event news in the Discord and on the event page. Check both during the event.

# 2. Teams and rosters

## 2.1 Signing up
2.1.1 Sign up your team on the event page before signups close.
2.1.2 A roster has 5 players, up to 2 subs and an optional coach.
2.1.3 Some events reserve places, for example for students. The event page says who qualifies and how to claim one.
2.1.4 Every player must be eligible under Code of conduct section 4, except as section 2.4.2 allows.
2.1.5 Staff can refuse a signup that doesn't meet the event's rules.
2.1.6 A team needs 5 eligible players on its roster to sign up.
2.1.7 Signing up after signups close needs staff approval.

## 2.2 Team names, tags and logos
2.2.1 Team names, tags and logos can't be offensive, can't copy another team's, and can't advertise gambling, betting or adult sites.
2.2.2 A team can carry a sponsor's name in its name or logo.
2.2.3 Staff can make a team change its name, tag or logo, and can list it under a neutral name until it does.
2.2.4 A team can change its name during an event only with staff approval.

## 2.3 Roster changes
2.3.1 Change your roster on the event page until it locks. The event page shows when it locks.
2.3.2 After the lock, changes need staff approval, for example an emergency sub.
2.3.3 Only players on your roster can play your matches. Playing anyone else can cost you a map, the match or your place in the event.
2.3.4 You can only play for one team in an event, unless staff and every team involved agree first.
[penalties: twoteams]
2.3.5 Only the captain can add or remove players. A player can leave a roster on their own.
2.3.6 To change captain, the current captain hands the role over. If they've left the team, staff name a new one.

## 2.4 League rosters
2.4.1 In a league, the captain names 3 core players at signup. A team left with fewer than 3 has 7 days to get back to 3. Its matches in that time are forfeited, and after 7 days staff disqualify it.
2.4.2 A core player can live outside WA. Subs and every other player must live in WA.
2.4.3 A core player who lives outside WA must play the LAN final in person. If they don't, staff disqualify the team.
2.4.4 League rosters lock halfway through the regular season, on the date shown on the event page.
2.4.5 A player who leaves a team can't play for another team in the league for 1 week.

## 2.5 Coaches
2.5.1 A team can have one coach. Only the coach on its roster can coach it in the server.
2.5.2 A coach must be linked and eligible under Code of conduct section 4, and can't coach while banned from events.
2.5.3 A coach can't play. To play, they must be changed to a sub on the roster.
2.5.4 A coach can coach only one team in an event.
2.5.5 A coach follows the same conduct rules as players. The team is responsible for its coach under Code of conduct section 8.5.

## 2.6 Withdrawing and disqualification
2.6.1 If your team is leaving an event, tell staff in a ticket.
2.6.2 A team that forfeits 2 matches by not playing them is treated as withdrawn, unless staff agree there were exceptional circumstances.
2.6.3 A withdrawn or disqualified team gets no prize. In a league or group stage, every match it played or was due to play becomes a forfeit win for its opponent. In a bracket, its played results stand and its remaining matches are forfeited.
2.6.4 Staff can ban players who caused a withdrawal, for example by leaving mid-season, from the next event.
2.6.5 Staff can disqualify a team for a serious breach by any of its players or its coach, or for repeated breaches.

# 3. Fees, refunds and prizes

3.1 If an event has an entry fee, it's paid on the event page. A captain can pay for the whole team.
3.2 Players who haven't paid by the deadline on the event page lose their place to the waitlist.
3.3 If WACS cancels an event, entry fees are refunded.
3.4 If your team withdraws more than 48 hours before the event starts, you can ask for a refund. After that, fees aren't refunded unless staff agree.
3.5 The prize pool and how it's split are on the event page.
3.6 Prizes are paid by bank transfer within 30 days of the event ending.
3.7 A team prize is split equally between the players on the team's roster at the end of the event. If the team asks before it's paid, staff can pay it to the captain or split it another way the players agree.
3.8 A player's share of a prize lapses if staff don't have their payment details within 60 days of the event ending.
3.9 Staff can hold a prize while a protest, review or appeal about the event is open.
3.10 A team that loses its placing after being paid, for example under Code of conduct section 6.6.2, returns the prize.

# 4. Draft events

4.1 In a draft event, players sign up on their own and captains draft them into teams.
4.2 The event page says whether the draft is snake, linear or auction, and whether players are split into tiers.
4.3 In an auction draft, each captain bids from a budget set on the event page.
4.4 Draft results are final. Once you're drafted, you play for that team for the whole event.
4.5 Staff can replace a drafted player who drops out.
4.6 Sign up with your real rank and history. Hiding how good you are to get a better tier is smurfing under Code of conduct section 3.3.
4.7 Staff choose the captains unless the event page says otherwise.
4.8 Drafted teams can't trade players.

# 5. Formats and seeding

5.1 The event page sets the format, for example round robin, groups, swiss, or single or double elimination, and whether each stage is Bo1, Bo3 or Bo5.
5.2 Staff seed teams from rankings, past results or a draw. Seeds are on the event page.
5.3 Seeds and brackets are final once the event starts, unless staff fix an error.
5.4 A team with a bye moves on to the next round. A bye isn't a match and doesn't count in standings.
5.5 In double elimination, the event page says whether the upper bracket team starts the grand final a map ahead.
5.6 Staff can change the format before the event starts if fewer teams sign up than planned.

# 6. Scheduling

## 6.1 Match weeks
6.1.1 Online leagues run in match weeks, Monday to Sunday.
6.1.2 Each match is set for the event's match night unless it's rescheduled.
6.1.3 Your match time is the one on the match page. All times are Perth time.
6.1.4 Staff can forfeit a match that isn't played in its match week, against the team that caused it. If both teams did, staff can record it as a loss for both.
6.1.5 Staff set playoff match times. A playoff match can only be moved with staff approval.

## 6.2 Rescheduling
6.2.1 Ask to move a match on the match page.
6.2.2 The new time must be in the same match week. Anything else needs staff approval.
6.2.3 A new time must be agreed at least 1 hour before the scheduled time. After that, only staff can move the match.
6.2.4 Answer a request within 24 hours. You can decline without a reason, but offer another time where you can.
6.2.5 Staff can move any match, for example for a clash or a server problem, and tell both teams when they do.
6.2.6 If the teams don't agree a new time, the original time stands.
6.2.7 A match can be played before its match week if both teams agree.

## 6.3 Punctuality
6.3.1 Be ready at the scheduled time. Your team is late if your captain or coach hasn't readied up for the veto by then.
6.3.2 A late team gets:
- a warning at 10 minutes late
- a map loss at 15 minutes, which is the match in a Bo1
- a match forfeit at 20 minutes
6.3.3 Staff confirm every map loss and forfeit, and can waive one for circumstances outside your control. Time lost to a veto or server fault doesn't count.
6.3.4 If your opponent is late, stay on the veto page until staff confirm what happens.
6.3.5 If both teams are late, staff decide what happens.
6.3.6 A team that doesn't turn up at all is treated as late.

# 7. Match procedure

7.1 When the veto ends, the server details appear on the match page. Only players in the match, the team's coach, casters and staff can join.
7.2 Join the server and ready up within 10 minutes of the veto ending. Staff can treat a team that isn't ready by then as late under section 6.3.
7.3 Warmup lasts until both teams are ready. Type .ready in chat once you're set. A team is ready when all 5 of its players have readied, or when one of them types .forceready with at least 4 in the server.
7.4 Staff can ready a team, or start the map, when a team is holding it up without a good reason.
7.5 Where a map's starting sides are decided by a knife round, the team with more players alive at the end wins it, then the team with more health left, then one chosen at random.
7.6 The knife round winner has 60 seconds to type .stay or .switch. If they don't, both teams stay on their current sides.
7.7 Once a map is live, stay in the server until it ends.
7.8 After each map, both teams ready up again in warmup. Be ready within 10 minutes of the previous map ending.
7.9 A team can change players between maps. During a map, only a disconnect under section 9.4 lets a sub come in.
7.10 In the server, use the name on your WACS profile under Code of conduct section 3.6.
7.11 Keep all chat during a live map to pauses, problems and good games.
7.12 A team that stops playing a live map without staff approval loses it, and staff can forfeit the match.
7.13 Teams can't agree between themselves to skip, replay or shorten a map. Only staff can.

# 8. Veto

8.1 The veto opens 30 minutes before the match on the match page. Each team's captain or coach picks the 5 players for the match and readies up, and the veto starts when both teams are ready.
8.2 In a league, the higher seed chooses whether to ban first or second. Otherwise the team listed first on the match page bans first.
8.3 In a Bo1, the teams ban in turn until one map is left.
8.4 In a Bo3, each team bans one map, then each picks one, then the teams ban in turn until one map is left. That map is the decider.
8.5 In a Bo5, the teams ban in turn until five maps are left, then pick in turn until one is left. That map is the decider.
8.6 The team that didn't pick a map chooses its starting side. A knife round decides sides on the decider, and on the only map of a Bo1. Some events use a knife round on every map, and the event page says so.
8.7 Some events set a time limit for each ban or pick. If it runs out, the choice is made at random.
8.8 Maps come from the map pool on the event page.
8.9 A ban or pick can't be undone, even if it was a mistake.
8.10 If the veto page fails, staff run the veto in the Discord in the same order.
8.11 The map pool stays the same for the whole event. If a game update changes or removes a map, staff announce what happens.

# 9. Technical rules

## 9.1 Game settings
9.1.1 Matches are MR12: first to 13 rounds wins a map.
9.1.2 A 12–12 tie goes to overtime. Overtime is MR3 with $10,000 start money, repeated until there's a winner.
9.1.3 Friendly fire and team collision are on.
9.1.4 Don't change server settings or call votes in the game's vote menu.
9.1.5 Teams can't agree between themselves to change match settings.
9.1.6 If a game update lands during an event, matches are played on the new version unless staff say otherwise.

## 9.2 Tactical pauses
9.2.1 Each team has 3 tactical pauses of 30 seconds per map, plus 1 more in each overtime.
9.2.2 Call one with .tac. It starts at the next freeze time.
9.2.3 Only the team that called a tactical pause can end it early.

## 9.3 Technical pauses
9.3.1 The event page says whether technical pauses are allowed. Where they aren't, pause with .tac and ask staff.
9.3.2 Technical pauses are for technical problems only. Call one with .tech and say why in all chat straight away.
9.3.3 A technical pause ends after 30 seconds. If you need longer, ask staff, who can hold the pause for up to 10 minutes.
9.3.4 Both teams type .unpause to end a technical pause, or staff end it.
9.3.5 Staff can ask for proof of a technical problem.
9.3.6 Pausing without a reason, or to break the other team's rhythm, is pause abuse. Staff can end the pause, and treat repeat abuse as a breach under section 10.5.
9.3.7 A team that still can't play after 10 minutes plays on with fewer players under section 9.4.3, or staff rule on the match.

## 9.4 Disconnects
9.4.1 The game doesn't pause when a player disconnects. Their team can pause at the next freeze time.
9.4.2 If a player disconnects before the first kill of a map's first round, staff restart the map once they're back.
9.4.3 After that, the team has up to 10 minutes for the player to return. Then it plays on with fewer players, or brings in a sub from its roster with staff approval.
9.4.4 A player who comes back can rejoin at the next freeze time.
9.4.5 A team needs at least 4 players in the server to start a map.
9.4.6 If a player keeps disconnecting, staff can ask the team to replace them between maps.

## 9.5 Server problems
9.5.1 If the server crashes, or a server or game fault breaks a round, staff restore the round from its backup. Score, money and equipment go back to the start of that round.
9.5.2 Only staff can restore a round. Pause and ask staff in the server or the Discord.
9.5.3 After a restore, both teams type .unpause when they're ready.
9.5.4 If a server can't be fixed, staff can move the match to another server and replay the map from its start, or reschedule it.
9.5.5 A problem with a player's own computer, connection or game isn't a server problem, and isn't a reason to replay a round.
9.5.6 If no backup can be restored, staff decide what happens from the GOTV demo and the score.

## 9.6 Bugs during a round
9.6.1 If a bug decides a round, for example the bomb can't be defused or a player is stuck, pause at the next freeze time and tell staff.
9.6.2 Staff can replay the round from its backup, or let it stand.
9.6.3 A bug that both teams could have used, or that didn't decide the round, isn't a reason to replay it.

## 9.7 Coaching
9.7.1 A coach can talk to their team during warmup, freeze time, pauses and between maps.
9.7.2 A coach can't talk to the team while a round is live.
[penalties: coaching]
9.7.3 A coach who talks during a live round can be removed from the server for the rest of the match.

## 9.8 Spectators in the server
9.8.1 Only casters, observers and staff can spectate in a match server.
9.8.2 Players and coaches from other teams can't join a match server, even as spectators.

# 10. Protests and rulings

10.1 A team can protest a match over a rule breach, an ineligible player, or a technical problem that changed the result.
10.2 During a live map, raise a problem by pausing at the next freeze time and telling staff. If staff say to play on, you can still protest afterwards.
10.3 If you knowingly play on without raising a problem, you can't protest it later. Cheating can be reported at any time.
10.4 To protest after a match, open a ticket within 24 hours of it ending. Say which match and which rule, describe what happened, and give the map and round if you can.
10.5 Staff can rule to:
- replay a round from its backup
- award a map to the other team
- award the match to the other team
- correct a score
- replay a map or the whole match
- disqualify a team under section 2.6
10.6 Staff tell both teams about a protest and the ruling, and which rule it was given under.
10.7 A result is final once the protest window closes, or once staff have ruled on every protest. Appeals follow Code of conduct section 9.2.
10.8 Raise a problem with the veto before the first map goes live.
10.9 A protest about an opponent's eligibility can be made before the match, or within 24 hours of it ending.
10.10 Staff can delay a team's next match while a protest about it is open.
10.11 A protest made in bad faith is misleading staff under Code of conduct section 5.3.2.

# 11. Results

11.1 Results appear on the match page when the match ends.
11.2 A forfeit counts as a win for the other team. In the standings it counts as the full map score, 1–0 in a Bo1 or 2–0 in a Bo3, with no rounds.
11.3 League and group standings rank teams by:
- wins
- wins against the other teams level on wins
- map difference
- round difference
- seed
11.4 Playoff seeding from a league or group follows the same order.
11.5 If a result doesn't appear on its own, staff enter it from the GOTV demo.
11.6 A replayed map or match replaces the original result.
11.7 If a ruling changes a result after a stage has ended, staff update the standings and decide whether later matches stand.

# 12. Broadcast

12.1 WACS holds the broadcast rights to its events, and can stream any match with casters.
12.2 You can stream your own point of view only with staff approval, and with the delay staff set.
[penalties: pov]
12.3 Casters and observers can't pass information to teams during a match.
12.4 Official streams run on a delay, so they can't be used to ghost.
12.5 A streamed match can start up to 15 minutes late for the broadcast. That time doesn't count as late under section 6.3.
12.6 Teams can't refuse to have a match streamed.

# 13. LAN events

## 13.1 Schedule and check-in
13.1.1 The match night, rescheduling and lateness rules in section 6 don't apply at a LAN. The event page sets the day's schedule, and matches run in bracket order.
13.1.2 Check in at the venue by the time on the event page, with every player and the coach.
13.1.3 Every player in a LAN match plays from the venue.
13.1.4 Be at your computers when your match is called. A team that isn't ready 10 minutes after the call loses a map, which is the match in a Bo1, and after 20 minutes forfeits the match.
13.1.5 Between maps, be back at your computers within 10 minutes unless the schedule gives a longer break.
13.1.6 If you're held up on the way, tell staff as soon as you can. Staff can move a match for a delay outside your control, but don't have to.
13.1.7 A team that hasn't checked in by the check-in time can lose its place to a reserve team.
13.1.8 Stay at the venue, or where staff can reach you by phone, until your team is out of the event or done for the day.
13.1.9 Staff can change the day's schedule. Changes are announced at the venue and in the Discord.
13.1.10 Each team gets at least 15 minutes between the end of one match and the start of its next.

## 13.2 Identity
13.2.1 Bring photo ID. Staff can check that each player is the person on the account they play on.
13.2.2 The venue's age rules apply on top of Code of conduct section 4.3, for example in a licensed area.
13.2.3 Players under 18 need written consent from a parent or guardian to attend, sent to staff in a ticket before the event.
13.2.4 A player who can't show photo ID when staff ask can be stopped from playing.

## 13.3 Rosters at the venue
13.3.1 A team plays its LAN matches with players on its roster. Changes at the event follow section 2.3.2.
13.3.2 If a player can't play, the team brings in a sub from its roster. If it has none at the venue, staff can let it play with 4, or approve an emergency sub who is eligible and isn't on another roster in the event.
13.3.3 A team with fewer than 4 players at the venue when its match is called is late under section 13.1.4.
13.3.4 A coach checks in like a player. A coach who isn't at the venue can't coach the team from anywhere else.

## 13.4 Equipment and setup
13.4.1 Bring your own mouse, keyboard, headset and mousepad. The event page says what the venue provides, such as computers, monitors and the network.
13.4.2 Peripherals follow Code of conduct section 6.3. Onboard macros and binds that make more than one action from one key are banned.
13.4.3 Wireless peripherals are allowed. Staff can ask you to switch to wired if one interferes with other players.
13.4.4 Install only drivers for your peripherals and your own game config. Bring your config on a USB drive or through Steam Cloud.
13.4.5 Don't change a venue computer's settings beyond your game settings, peripheral drivers and display settings.
13.4.6 You get 15 minutes at your computer before your team's first match to set up and test.
13.4.7 Staff can check computers and peripherals before or after any match under Code of conduct section 7.4.
13.4.8 Play on your own Steam account, and sign out of it when your team is done with the computer.
13.4.9 Venue computers can run anti-cheat or monitoring software that staff choose. Turning it off or getting around it is cheating under Code of conduct section 6.1.1.
13.4.10 Use venue computers only for your matches and your setup.
13.4.11 Look after your own equipment. WACS isn't responsible for anything lost, stolen or damaged at the venue.

## 13.5 Problems at the venue
13.5.1 If a venue computer, monitor or the network fails during a map, call a technical pause. Staff handle it as a server problem under section 9.5.
13.5.2 If your own peripheral fails, fix or replace it during a pause. That isn't a reason to replay a round.
13.5.3 Staff can move a team to another computer or station between maps.
13.5.4 Technical pauses are always allowed at a LAN, whatever the event page says about online matches.
13.5.5 A team has up to 10 minutes to replace a failed peripheral. After that, it plays on.

## 13.6 Communication
13.6.1 Use your headset for team voice during a live map. Only your team, and your coach under section 9.7, can be on it.
13.6.2 Phones and other devices stay away from your computer during a live map, except a device staff approve for team voice.
13.6.3 Don't talk to spectators or anyone outside your team during a live map. Doing so is ghosting under Code of conduct section 6.4.1.
13.6.4 Keep your headset on for the whole of a live map.
13.6.5 Keep your voice low enough that the other team can't hear you, and don't listen to them.
13.6.6 Staff can listen to and record team voice during a LAN match. Recordings are used only for rulings.
13.6.7 During a live map, only the player at a computer touches it. A coach stays behind their team.

## 13.7 Conduct at the venue
13.7.1 Follow the venue's rules, including on alcohol, food and smoking.
13.7.2 Keep food and drink off the playing tables. Staff can ask you to move them.
13.7.3 Spectators can watch from behind players. If it's a distraction, call a pause and tell staff.
13.7.4 At a café, other customers may be around. Staff will raise a disruptive customer with the venue, but can't promise to resolve it.
13.7.5 Staff can remove anyone from the venue for behaviour under Code of conduct section 5. A player who damages venue equipment is responsible for the damage.
13.7.6 WACS can photograph and film players at the venue and publish it under Code of conduct section 10.1.
13.7.7 Only players in the current match, their coaches and staff can be in the player area.
13.7.8 Staff can stop a player who is drunk or affected by drugs from playing.
13.7.9 Don't taunt opponents at their computers.
13.7.10 The event page can set a dress code, such as team shirts.

## 13.8 Protests and rulings
13.8.1 At a LAN, raise a protest with staff before the next map starts. After a team's last map, raise it within 15 minutes.
13.8.2 Staff rule at the venue before the bracket moves on.
13.8.3 An appeal of a match ruling at a LAN is heard at the venue by a member of staff who wasn't involved. Their decision is final.
13.8.4 Penalties against a player, such as event bans, can still be appealed after the event under Code of conduct section 9.2.

## 13.9 Emergencies
13.9.1 In an emergency, follow staff and venue instructions first. Staff pause every match.
13.9.2 After a power cut, a network failure or an evacuation, matches restart from their backups under section 9.5, or staff reschedule them.
13.9.3 If the event can't finish on the day, staff decide how it ends, such as on a later date, online, or by splitting the remaining prizes.

## 13.10 Travel
13.10.1 Travel and accommodation are at your own cost.
13.10.2 If a LAN is moved or cancelled, entry fees are refunded under section 3.3. Travel and accommodation costs aren't.
